**Abstract** *(original abstract, verbatim from PubMed)*

> BACKGROUND: Distributional concerns have gained increasing attention in health technology assessment, complementing traditional cost-effectiveness methods. Among the proposed equity-sensitive frameworks, the equivalent income approach evaluates health interventions by jointly considering health, income, healthcare financing, and societal aversion to inequality. However, its implementation requires additional data and methodological developments. OBJECTIVE: We aimed to provide a practical tutorial for implementing the equivalent income approach in health technology assessment and to compare its recommendations with those derived from conventional cost-effectiveness analysis. METHODS: We developed a generic individual-level Markov model comparing two hypothetical treatment strategies over a 30-year horizon in a representative sample of the French population. For each individual and cycle, the model estimates health status, income, healthcare costs, willingness-to-pay for health improvements, and health insurance contributions. Individual equivalent incomes are aggregated using a social welfare function incorporating different levels of inequality aversion. In parallel, a conventional cost-effectiveness analysis based on discounted health outcomes and costs was performed. RESULTS: Whereas cost-effectiveness analysis recommendations depended solely on treatment effectiveness and costs, the equivalent income approach additionally accounted for the distribution of disease burden, healthcare financing arrangements, and societal preferences regarding inequality. Reimbursement recommendations differed substantially between the two approaches, particularly when disadvantaged populations were disproportionately affected by the disease or exempted from healthcare contributions. CONCLUSIONS: The equivalent income approach extends conventional cost-effectiveness analysis by explicitly incorporating distributive concerns into health technology assessment. Beyond comparing recommendations, this tutorial provides a transparent and reproducible framework for operationalizing equivalent income-based evaluations and supporting their future use in routine health technology assessment.

**Abstract** *(original abstract, verbatim from PubMed)*

> BACKGROUND: Breast cancer (BC) is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide and poses a major public health and economic challenge in Egypt, where late-stage diagnosis remains high. Egypt launched the Presidential Initiative for Women's Health, a nationwide, publicly funded program providing free early detection, diagnostic, and treatment services. This study presents a model-based economic evaluation of the initiative. METHODS: A hybrid decision tree and Markov model compared the Presidential Initiative pathway with opportunistic case detection, from a societal perspective over a lifetime horizon. Outcomes were quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), life years, net societal cost, and return on investment (ROI); health outcomes were not monetized. Model structure was assessed using face, internal, and cross-validation following the ISPOR-SMDM taxonomy with structured review by 16 clinical and program co-authors. Uncertainty was examined using Monte Carlo probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) with 5000 iterations. Inputs were national program data for July 2019 to September 2024. RESULTS: Approximately 23.8 million unique women generated 53,453,745 recorded screening and service encounters. Among confirmed cases, the observed stage I to II proportion was 61.3% in the initiative data versus 41.6% in the comparator. The model estimated 32,576 incremental QALYs and 63,448 additional discounted life years, with total societal costs of EGP 11.68 billion (USD 258 million) versus EGP 18.07 billion (USD 398 million), a net societal saving of EGP 6.39 billion (USD 141 million) and an ROI of 54.7%. In the PSA, at the Egyptian threshold of EGP 164,588 per QALY, the initiative was cost-effective in all 5,000 simulations (100%), and dominant in 4,988 (99.76%), with a base-case net monetary benefit of EGP 11.75 billion (USD 259 million). CONCLUSIONS: Under the observed program stage distribution, the Presidential Initiative was projected to provide greater modeled health outcomes at lower societal cost than opportunistic case detection, dominant in the base-case and cost-effective at the Egyptian threshold in every simulation. These are model-based estimates conditional on transferred survival and utility inputs. The findings support continued program evaluation and investment alongside collection of Egyptian long-term outcome data.

**Abstract** *(original abstract, verbatim from PubMed)*

> UNLABELLED: Osteoporosis often goes undetected until a fracture occurs. Using a deep learning model to analyze routine chest radiographs may cost-effectively identify at-risk individuals. Economic benefit depends on how common the disease is, how the model is calibrated, and whether patients follow through with confirmatory bone density testing and pharmacological treatment initiation. BACKGROUND: Osteoporosis is common among postmenopausal women and often unrecognized until fragility fractures occur, despite its clinical and economic burden. Deep learning (DL)-based opportunistic screening using existing chest radiographs is a potential approach, but its economic value may vary with disease prevalence, diagnostic performance, and care pathway completion. AIM: To assess the economic value of a DL-based opportunistic osteoporosis screening approach among Taiwanese postmenopausal women. METHODS: A cost-utility analysis was conducted from the Taiwan healthcare perspective using a hybrid decision tree and Markov model. The model simulated a cohort of 50-year-old postmenopausal women over a 50-year horizon. Model inputs, including diagnostic performance, long-term outcomes, costs, and utilities, were derived from published literature, preferably local sources. Additional analyses examined alternative prevalence settings, sensitivity and specificity combinations, and confirmatory DXA and treatment initiation rates. RESULTS: In the base case, assuming full care-pathway completion, DL-based screening was cost-effective versus no screening, with an ICER of 21,827 per QALY, below the willingness-to-pay threshold of one GDP per capita in Taiwan. The approach became cost-effective when osteoporosis prevalence exceeded 14.3%. Across the diagnostic continuum, a sensitivity of 86.6% and specificity of 76.3% generated the greatest economic benefit. CONCLUSION: DL-based opportunistic osteoporosis screening was cost-effective under full care-pathway implementation. Its value depended on disease prevalence, diagnostic performance, and completion of confirmatory DXA and treatment initiation, and was not maintained under real-world examination estimates observed in Taiwan. Strengthening the post-screening care pathway is therefore central to realizing this value.
