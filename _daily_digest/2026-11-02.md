**Abstract** *(original abstract, verbatim from medRxiv)*

> IntroductionNatural history models must often be estimated from data that do not directly capture disease transitions. Semi-Markov processes let the risk of progression depend on the time spent in a health state, which Markov models need extra states to represent. Their limitation is computational: discretizing time introduces approximation error, and simulating individuals introduces Monte Carlo error and a non-differentiable calibration objective. MethodsWe show that the model-implied prevalence, incidence and cause-specific mortality of a continuous-time semi-Markov model satisfy convolution equations that become a small linear system when Laplace-transformed. Solving it on a grid of complex frequencies and inverting with an accelerated Fourier series returns the targets without a time grid or Monte Carlo error, and yields exact first- and second-order derivatives for one additional linear solve each. These support Newton-type maximum likelihood estimation, gradient-informed Bayesian sampling, and local identifiability diagnostics. We apply our open-source implementation to a urinary bladder cancer model calibrated to SEER incidence and mortality by age and stage. ResultsThe method reproduced an independent implementation to within its accuracy (maximum relative difference 8 x 10-6) and evaluated all targets, their gradient, and their Hessian in 1.3, 2.5, and 9.1 milliseconds; a one-year-cycle cohort approximation deviated by up to 42% and a two-million-person microsimulation by up to 811%. Maximum likelihood and Bayesian estimates agreed, and second-order information showed that the aggregate targets constrain where individuals go from preclinical states far better than how long they stay. Early-stage incidence and mortality showed systematic misfit across age. ConclusionsThe framework supplies existing calibration algorithms with fast, exact model evaluations and derivatives for models whose transition hazards depend on time in state rather than on age. > Numerical simulation models that accurately predict the behavior of complex materials such as the aerospace-grade Ti-6Al-4V are often highly computationally expensive and contain a large number of model parameters that must be inferred by comparing the model predictions with experimental observations. Often, such calibration is carried out using deterministic optimization procedures, but these methods do not account for physical, data, or modeling uncertainties and may produce results that cannot be meaningfully extrapolated to new operating conditions not covered by the calibration experiments. On the other hand, uncertainty-aware calibration using the Bayesian framework is more robust, but it is difficult to carry out for a large number of parameters because of the curse of dimensionality and the computational expense of evaluating the simulation model many times. With this context, the contributions of this work are twofold. First, we investigate the application of Bayesian calibration, enhanced with active subspace-based dimension reduction and surrogate modeling, to a crystal plasticity finite element model that predicts the mechanical properties of a forged Ti-6Al-4V alloy. In particular, we demonstrate how dimension reduction can enable calibration in high dimensions without the use of sophisticated sampling techniques or heuristic down-selecting of parameters. Next, we study the impact of the latent space dimension (constructed through dimension reduction) on the calibrated posteriors. Most importantly, we examine how optimal single-value estimates of the model parameters differ between deterministic calibration algorithms and Bayesian point estimates, underscoring the necessity of appropriately incorporating all physical, modeling, and approximation uncertainties to ensure robust calibration outcomes.

**Abstract** *(original abstract, verbatim from OpenAlex (reconstructed from its inverted index))*

> Patients with platinum-resistant recurrent ovarian cancer (PRROC) have limited therapeutic options. Pembrolizumab plus paclitaxel with optional bevacizumab (pembrolizumab group) has recently emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for this population. Nevertheless, the high acquisition and treatment costs associated with this regimen raise important concerns regarding its economic value, making a formal cost-effectiveness evaluation essential. A three-state Markov model with a 15-year time horizon was constructed using clinical efficacy data from the KEYNOTE-B96 trial together with treatment-related cost inputs for estimating lifetime costs and health outcomes for PRROC patients with PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 1 receiving either the pembrolizumab regimen or placebo. Cost-effectiveness was assessed using willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds of \$150,000/QALY for the USA and \$40,500/QALY for China. Sensitivity analyses showed that when pembrolizumab prices were reduced by 10% in the USA and 30% in China, the ICERs declined to levels below the respective WTP thresholds. At current market prices, pembrolizumab plus paclitaxel with optional bevacizumab does not represent a cost-effective option for PRROC. However, price reductions for pembrolizumab could substantially improve its economic attractiveness.
